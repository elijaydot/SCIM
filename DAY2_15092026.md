Authentication and Authorization
Two concepts are fundamental to understanding identity and access.

Authentication
- Identifies the person or service seeking access to a resource.
- Requests legitimate access credentials.
- Forms the basis for creating secure identity and access control principles.

Authorization
- Determines an authenticated person’s or service’s level of access.
- Defines which data they can access and what they can do with it.

NB: Authentication comes first, while Authorization follows

Azure Active Directory
This is where identities live:
    Users
    Apps
    Groups

Think of it as the school's list of students and staff.

Azure Subscription
A subscription is like the whole school building.
RBAC can control who can access it.

Resource Group
A resource group is like a classroom inside the school.
You can give someone access to:
    - The whole school (Subscription)
    - Or just one classroom (Resource Group)

Real-Life Example
Imagine you're playing Minecraft with friends.

Friend 1: Builder
Can:
    - Build houses
Cannot:
    - Ban players

Friend 2: Moderator

    - Kick troublemakers

    - Change server settings

Friend 3: Server Owner
Can:
    - Do everything

That's RBAC!
Everyone gets permissions based on their role.

    RBAC = Right People + Right Access + Right Resources

    RBAC decides "who can do what" in Azure. 🔐🛡️

Key Points
- Protect your Azure resources from accidental deletion or modification.
- Manage locks at the subscription, resource group, or individual resource levels within the Azure portal.

What the Lock Types Mean
CanNotDelete
- You can read the resource.
- You can update (change) the resource.
- You cannot delete the resource.

ReadOnly
- You can read the resource.
- You cannot update the resource.
- You cannot delete the resource.

This lock is more restrictive because it prevents both changes and deletion.

📚 Further Reading
🛡️ IRM (Information Rights Management)

Imagine you lend your notebook to a friend, but you put special rules on it:
    They can read it ✅
    They cannot copy it ❌
    They cannot print it ❌
    They cannot share it with others ❌

IRM does the same thing for digital files and emails.

Real-life example:
A company's HR team sends employee salary information.
With IRM, employees can:
    Read the email ✅
But they cannot:
    Forward it ✅❌
    Print it ✅❌
    Copy the contents ✅❌

The owner controls what others can do with the information.

🔒 AIP (Azure Information Protection)
Imagine all your school books have stickers:

Red sticker = Top Secret
Yellow sticker = Teacher Only
Green sticker = Everyone

AIP helps companies put these "stickers" on files and emails so everyone knows how sensitive they are.

Real-life example:
A finance manager creates a spreadsheet with company profits.

Using AIP, they mark it:
    "Confidential - Finance Team Only"

Now:
    The file can be encrypted.
    Only Finance Team members can open it.
    If someone outside the team receives it, they cannot read it.

Note: AIP capabilities are included in licenses such as Microsoft 365 E5.

🏷️ Sensitivity Labels
Think of labels on lunch boxes:
    My Lunch
    Family Lunch
    School Lunch

The label tells everyone how the item should be treated.

Sensitivity Labels tell people and computers how to protect information.

Real-life example:
An employee creates a document containing customer data.

They choose a label:
        Confidential

As a result:
    The document gets encrypted.
    Sharing may be restricted.
    Watermarks may appear.

The label automatically protects the document.

📧 Transport Rules (Mail Flow Rules)
Imagine a school post office.

Whenever someone sends a letter with the word "Exam Answers," the teacher automatically puts a red "Secret" stamp on it.

That's what transport rules can do for emails.

Real-life example:
A company creates a rule:
    If an email contains credit card numbers, automatically apply the "Confidential" sensitivity label.

Now every matching email gets protected automatically without the sender doing anything.

🔍 SIT (Sensitive Information Type)
Imagine a robot looking through papers for:
    Phone numbers
    ID numbers
    Credit card numbers

When it finds them, it raises its hand and says:
    "I found sensitive information!"

That's a Sensitive Information Type.

Real-life example:
An employee emails:
    Credit Card: 4111-1111-1111-1111

Microsoft detects the credit card pattern.

The SIT identifies:
    Credit card numbers
    Passport numbers
    National IDs
    Bank account numbers

Then security policies can protect the data.

📊 MSEM (Microsoft Security Exposure Management)
Imagine you're checking your house for weak spots:
    Open windows
    Unlocked doors
    Broken fences

You fix the biggest problems first.

MSEM helps organizations find and prioritize security weaknesses before attackers can exploit them.

Real-life example:
A company has:
    200 devices
    500 users
    Thousands of files

MSEM identifies:
    Devices missing security updates
    Users with risky permissions
    Critical vulnerabilities

Then it tells security teams:
    "Fix these high-risk problems first."

🏆 Microsoft Secure Score (Microsoft Defender)
Think of a video game score.
The more security tasks you complete, the higher your score.
A higher score usually means you're following more security best practices.

Real-life example:
A company starts with:
    Secure Score = 45%

Microsoft recommends:
    Enable MFA ✅
    Protect admin accounts ✅
    Configure email protection ✅

After completing these actions:
    Secure Score = 82%

The score helps the company see how well its environment is protected and what improvements are still needed.
